Sthapatya — the building of it

One owner per idea

Any number computed twice will disagree eventually. When a bug needs four fixes at four call sites, that isn't a hard bug — it's a value with no owner. I'd rather ship three similar lines than one abstraction invented before it was needed.

04

The path

Not a list of jobs — the four times the question I was being asked changed. The tools beside each one are the honest record of what a decade actually looks like.

  • 2021 — Present · Robosoft · Bengaluru, remote

    Scale changed the question

    One shared source, many shipping brands — so every choice I made was a choice made on behalf of teams I will never meet. The question stopped being “does this work” and became “what does this cost whoever inherits it.”

    The codebase taught me more than any course could. Feature flags, design tokens, XcodeGen, interactors and templates, dependency injection, an AppObserver pattern that keeps AppDelegate almost empty — reading code that good is its own apprenticeship.

    Swift 6 · SwiftUI · tvOS · visionOS · XcodeGen · Sonar Cloud

  • 2019 — 2020 · Bajaj Markets · Pune

    Money on the other side

    The first place a mistake would cost someone money rather than a session. Convenience stopped being a neutral word to me here — a shortcut through an auth boundary is a promise you are making on the user's behalf, whether or not you meant to.

    Deep Linking · CMS-driven layout · WebView auth · Jira

  • 2018 — 2019 · Muvi · Bhubaneswar

    Building for builders

    Muvi's customers stood up their own streaming services on what we shipped. Writing for other developers instead of end users is the first time an awkward API stops being your opinion and becomes someone else's whole afternoon.

    Swift · Alamofire · MVVM · Crashlytics · Unit Tests

  • 2016 — 2018 · Mobiona · Bhubaneswar

    Where it started

    A services shop, so a lot of work came across the desk — Objective-C giving way to Swift, one project at a time. This is where I hit the things nobody teaches you: project-file conflicts, why code needs a repository at all, animation timing, and SOAP and XML that did not want to be parsed.

    I stayed because of what I could see underneath it. The tools for building here, and the standard the platform holds you to, were better than anything else I had touched — and every piece of knowledge made the next piece easier. It compounds. That is the whole reason I am still at it.

    Objective-C · UIKit · Core Data · Realm · Storyboards · SOAP / XML
